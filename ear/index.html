INTERACTIVE / EAR TRAINING

Hear a note.
Know what it is.

Most musicians don't have "perfect pitch", and they don't need it. They hear how far a note is from home. This page trains exactly that: first you listen and learn what each scale degree, interval and chord feels like, then you test yourself in the gym until naming them becomes automatic. Headphones help.

01 / HOW TO LISTEN

Relative pitch: measure from home.

You name a note by comparing it with a note you already know: the key's home note, the note before it, or an open string. Train the comparison and every note becomes recognisable.

Sing everything

If you can sing it, you can hear it. Hum each note after the page plays it, before you answer. It is the fastest way to connect ear and brain.

Feel, don't calculate

Each degree has a feeling: 1 is home, 7 aches to go up, 4 leans down. Learn the feelings in sections 02–04 and answers will come without counting.

Little and often

Ten minutes a day beats an hour a week. The ear improves while you sleep on it. Use the plan in section 07.

Your anchor notes: the open strings

These six notes are always under your fingers. Play them until you can hum each one from memory. Mnemonic, low to high: Eddie Ate Dynamite, Good Bye Eddie.

02 / SCALE DEGREES

Every note has a job in the key.

Play the chords of a key and your ear locks onto its home note. From then on, every other note has a feeling and a direction it wants to move. Learn these seven feelings and you can name any note in any key.

03 / INTERVALS

The distance between two notes.

An interval is measured in semitones (frets). Each one has its own colour, and the start of a famous tune to remember it by. Each card shows the interval on the neck from a C on the A string: along one string, and as the usual cross-string shape.

Song hooks are memory aids: use a tune you know well. The hook marks the first two notes of the melody unless noted. If a hook doesn't work for you, swap in your own.

04 / CHORD SOUNDS

Happy, sad, tense, dreamy.

Chord qualities are easier to hear than you think, because each has an emotional colour. Play each one strummed and then note by note, and listen for the word on the card.

05 / TRAINING GYM

Test yourself.

Pick a drill. Start at level 1. When you get 10 right in a row, move up a level. After each answer you'll hear why: the resolution, the interval hook or the chord formula.

06 / NOTE DETECTOR

Play a note. See its name.

Turn on the microphone and play (or sing) a single note. The detector names it, shows how in tune it is, and lights up every place it lives on the neck. Use it to check your answers, tune up, or explore the fretboard by ear.

The microphone is only used in this page and nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

07 / PROGRESS & PLAN

Ten minutes a day.

Your scores are saved in this browser. Follow the plan: a little of everything, every day, moving up a level when a drill feels easy.

DAILY ROUTINE · 10 MINUTES

The session

  1. 1 min · Play and hum the six open strings (section 01).
  2. 3 min · Scale degrees drill. The most useful skill of all.
  3. 2 min · Intervals drill, at your current level.
  4. 2 min · Chord quality drill.
  5. 2 min · Find it on the neck or Play it back, with your guitar in your hands.

A 6-WEEK PATH

What to aim for

  1. Week 1 · Higher/lower level 3, scale degrees level 1, major vs minor chords.
  2. Week 2 · Scale degrees level 2, intervals level 1.
  3. Week 3 · Scale degrees level 3 (all seven), intervals level 2, chords level 2.
  4. Week 4 · Find it on the neck, Play it back with the detector.
  5. Week 5 · Intervals level 3 in all directions, chords level 3 (7ths).
  6. Week 6 · Chromatic scale degrees and all intervals. Start working out simple melodies from songs by ear.
Beyond the page
  • Work out melodies: pick a nursery rhyme or a chorus you know, find the first note on the guitar with the detector, then find each next note by ear.
  • Hear the bass: in songs you listen to, hum the lowest note of each chord. That's the root. Find it on your low E or A string.
  • Name the key: when a song ends, hum its final note. That is almost always "1", the home note.
