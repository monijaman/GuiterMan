PRACTICE / GUITAR TECHNIQUE & CARE

Clear notes.
Comfortable hands.

Make your playing cleaner and more expressive with focused exercises. Then learn the essentials of keeping your guitar ready to play.

01 / TECHNIQUE & CARE

Settle the guitar. Free your hands.

Good technique starts with a stable instrument and small, relaxed movements. Find a position that works for your body and instrument.

Support the instrument

Sit on a stable chair or use a strap. Bring the guitar to a comfortable height, with the neck slightly raised if that helps. The guitar should stay in place without your fretting hand holding it up.

Use only enough pressure

Place your fingertip just behind the fret wire. Pick a note, gradually reduce pressure until it buzzes, then add just enough to clear it. Keep the thumb mobile rather than squeezing constantly.

Keep movements small

Avoid a sharply folded wrist. Let shoulders settle, keep the pick grip light and use a mirror or short recording to check for excess movement. Pause and reset when you notice tension.

Prepare for a change

Move fingers close to the strings, then release pressure before shifting. Practise a difficult change slowly on its own before putting it back into the song.

One-minute check

Hold a single note at fret 5, pick it four times, release, then repeat on another string. Look for a clear note and a relaxed hand, not maximum volume.

02 / TECHNIQUE & CARE

Keep the notes you want. Quiet the rest.

Lead clarity depends on both hands controlling unused strings. Noise becomes much easier to hear with distortion, so learn the movement with a clean sound first.

Picking-hand control

Let the side of your palm lightly touch the lower-pitched strings you are not using. Move the contact point as you change strings. Keep it off a string that should sustain.

Fretting-hand control

A fretting finger can lightly touch an adjacent higher-pitched string without pressing it to a fret. Other free fingers can help silence unused strings. Check that the intended note still rings.

Palm-muted tone

For a deliberate short, bass-heavy sound, rest the palm edge near the bridge on the string you play. Move a little farther from the bridge for stronger damping. This changes the tone, unlike simply silencing unused strings.

End the note cleanly

Release fretting pressure but keep light contact to stop a fretted note. Use a free finger or picking hand to stop an open string. A rest is part of the phrase, so silence should begin on time.

Progression: repeat on G and high e, then play a short lead phrase with a rest between each answer. Listen for ringing strings during the gaps.

03 / TECHNIQUE & CARE

Alternate picking with a steady pulse.

Alternate picking means down–up–down–up. Use a small motion and let only a little pick tip cross the string. Start slowly enough that each attack sounds even.

Exercise 1 · One string

Play high e frets 5–7–8–7 twice: eight eighth notes. Keep the down–up sequence through the turnaround. Say the counts aloud and compare the volume of each stroke.

Exercise 2 · Cross strings

Play B-string frets 5–6–8, then high e fret 5. Repeat for eight notes. The first four strokes are down–up–down–up; keep the same pattern when returning to B.

Exercise 3 · A melody

Choose one bar from Lead Composing. Keep its written rhythm; practise the string changes separately. For rests, plan the next stroke before restarting instead of rushing the entrance.

When to raise the tempo

Aim for three clean repetitions with even timing and quiet unused strings. Raise the metronome by a small step, such as 4 BPM. If the movement gets tense or uneven, return to the previous tempo.

04 / TECHNIQUE & CARE

Bend to a pitch. Vibrato around a pitch.

A bend has a destination; vibrato is a controlled, repeated pitch movement. Learn them separately before combining them.

Support the bend

For this exercise, fret B7 with the ring finger and let the middle and index fingers support behind it. Use a comfortable hand rotation to move the string toward the bass side. Mute neighbouring strings as you move.

Match before decorating

Play B9, then B7 bent up a whole tone. Compare by ear or use the microphone pitch detector in Ear Training. Repeat slowly. If a whole-tone bend is difficult on your strings, begin with a smaller half-tone target, B7 to the pitch of B8.

Controlled release

Hold the target briefly, then release smoothly to the original pitch. If you want a silent release, mute the string before relaxing the bend. Listen for accidental ringing from the neighbouring strings.

Vibrato in time

Hold B5 (E), then make small bends and returns to the starting pitch. At 60 BPM, try one gentle bend-and-return per beat, then two. Keep depth and timing consistent; do not let every cycle drift farther sharp.

Apply it musically

Choose one held note in a composition. Play it plain first, then add subtle vibrato after the note settles. Avoid changing every note; one expressive ending can be enough.

05 / TECHNIQUE & CARE

A playable guitar makes practice easier.

Tuning, fresh strings and an appropriate setup help notes sound clear. Start by identifying the problem before adjusting anything.

Change strings methodically

Use a string set intended for your guitar type. Loosen a string before removing it, secure the replacement at the bridge and tuner using the method for that hardware, then bring it gradually to pitch. Locking tuners, classical ties and tremolo bridges need different procedures.

Retune and settle

For a first change, replacing one string at a time makes the process easier to follow, especially with a floating tremolo. Retune all six strings repeatedly as the new set settles. Wipe strings with a dry cloth after playing.

Know the setup terms

Action is string height above the frets. Relief is the slight curve of the neck. Intonation is how accurately notes stay in tune across the neck. A fret buzz or difficult grip can have several causes; changing one setting can affect another.

Check intonation

Tune the open string accurately. Compare its 12th-fret harmonic with the lightly fretted 12th-fret note: both should have the same pitch. Excess fretting pressure can make a note sharp, so repeat gently before judging the setup.

Setup limits: consult the guide for your exact guitar and bridge before making adjustments. The truss rod controls neck relief; it is not a general fix for every action or tuning problem. A technician can assess nut slots, fret condition and saddle geometry together.

06 / TECHNIQUE & CARE

Put the skills into a short practice session.

Use a clear goal for each block and listen back to one take. Finishing a small phrase cleanly is a useful measure of progress.

2 minutes · Tune & settle

Tune all strings, check your position, and play a few notes with minimum pressure.

3 minutes · Silence & attack

Do the note–rest muting drill, then the one-string alternate-picking drill. Keep unused strings quiet.

3 minutes · Pitch & expression

Compare a bend with its target note, then hold a plain note and add measured vibrato.

5 minutes · Make music

Open a lead composition. Select one bar, slow it down, and loop it. Add the backing chords once the notes and rhythm are comfortable.

Record one pass

Listen for timing, unwanted strings and pitch. Choose one thing to improve on the next pass, then play the entire phrase once without stopping.
